In-House vs. Agency: When Is the Right Time for a London Startup to Outsource Social Media?

Should your London startup hire in-house or outsource social media? Discover the key signs, costs, benefits and when an agency is the smarter choice for growth.

August 18, 2026BY BIG BEANS DIGITAL
Summarize with AI

For an early-stage London startup, social media can feel deceptively simple.

You create a LinkedIn post. Someone takes a few photos. The founder records a quick video. A team member publishes something on Instagram when they have five spare minutes.

Then the startup grows.

When should a London startup outsource social media?

Suddenly, social media is no longer just about posting. You need a consistent content strategy, stronger creative, community management, paid campaigns, analytics, brand positioning and a clear connection between social activity and commercial growth.

That is usually when the in-house vs agency social media question appears.

Should you hire a social media manager? Build an entire internal team? Continue managing it yourself? Or partner with a social media agency for startups?

For most London startups, the answer isn't simply about company size. It comes down to stage, budget, internal expertise, content requirements and growth ambitions.

And sometimes, outsourcing social media earlier than expected can be the smarter move.

01

Why Social Media Matters for London Startups

Social media has become a serious business channel rather than simply a place to build brand awareness.

DataReportal reported 54.8 million active social media user identities in the UK in January 2025, equivalent to 79% of the country's population.

Research from iwoca also found that 97% of UK SMEs use social media to help their business, with Facebook and Instagram among the most popular platforms.

For a London startup competing for attention in a crowded market, that creates both an opportunity and a challenge.

Your potential customers are already on social platforms.

Establish founder and brand authority
Generate awareness in a competitive market
Drive website traffic and enquiries
Create communities around products and services
Support recruitment and employer branding
Retarget potential customers through paid social
Turn customer stories into social proof

But none of this happens simply because a business has an Instagram or LinkedIn account.

Strategy matters.

02

In-House vs. Agency Social Media: What's the Difference?

At a basic level, the difference is straightforward.

In-House

In-house social media means your employees manage the strategy, content, publishing, engagement and reporting internally.

The biggest advantage is proximity.

Agency

Agency social media means an external specialist team handles some or all of those responsibilities.

The biggest advantage is broader specialist capability.

Neither model is automatically better. The right choice depends on what your startup needs at its current stage.

What an In-House Social Media Team Offers

Your employees understand your product, customers, culture and internal conversations every day. They can quickly access information from sales, product and leadership teams.

One social media hire rarely covers everything. You may need strategy, copywriting, graphic design, video editing, paid advertising, analytics, community management and platform expertise.

03

When Does It Make Sense to Keep Social Media In-House?

Outsourcing isn't always the answer. There are situations where building an internal social media function makes more sense.

01

You Have a Dedicated Marketing Team

If your startup already has a capable marketing department, adding social media responsibilities internally can be logical. You may already have a marketing lead, content resources, designers, video capability, paid media expertise and analytics support.

02

Your Brand Requires Constant Internal Input

Some businesses need frequent access to product teams, customer support or technical experts. A rapidly evolving SaaS startup, for example, may need its social team to understand product releases and technical developments almost immediately.

03

Social Is Already a Major Acquisition Channel

If social media is directly responsible for a substantial portion of your leads or sales, internal ownership may eventually become valuable. At that point, social isn't just a marketing task. It is a core business function.

04

You Have Enough Content Volume to Justify Internal Resources

If your startup needs a large volume of content every week, the economics can change. An internal content team may become more efficient when there is enough ongoing work to keep those employees productive.

The important point is that you should hire based on workload and business requirements, not simply because your startup has reached a certain number of employees.

04

When Should a London Startup Outsource Social Media?

For many startups, outsourcing makes sense before hiring a full internal social media team.

Here are the strongest signals.

1

Your Founder Is Still Doing the Posting

Founders often start by handling social media themselves because nobody else knows the business as well. But as the company grows, the founder's time becomes increasingly valuable. If you're spending several hours each week writing posts, editing videos, responding to comments and checking analytics, ask yourself: Is this really the highest-value use of my time?

2

Your Content Has Become Inconsistent

One week you publish four posts. The next week, nothing. Then you suddenly post three times because someone remembers that the account exists. An outsourced social media team can create a repeatable content workflow and editorial calendar so content doesn't depend on whether your founder or marketing manager has a free afternoon.

3

You Need Specialist Skills Without Multiple Hires

A growing startup might need strategy, copy, design, video, paid social, analytics and community management. Hiring one person for each discipline can become expensive very quickly. An agency lets you access multiple specialists through one relationship.

4

Your Social Media Isn't Producing Measurable Results

Likes are not a business strategy. If your startup has been posting consistently but cannot identify which content generates website visits, enquiries, audience engagement, leads or opportunities, your problem may not be a lack of content. It may be a lack of strategy and measurement.

5

You Need to Scale Quickly

You might close a major client, raise funding, enter a new market or launch a new product. Suddenly, you need more content and greater visibility. An agency can often increase capacity more quickly because the people, processes and tools are already in place.

6

Your Marketing Team Is Overloaded

Your marketing manager may already be responsible for email marketing, SEO, website updates, events, CRM, content, partnerships and reporting. Adding social media on top can turn an important growth channel into an afterthought.

7

You Don't Have Enough Strategic Expertise

Posting regularly isn't the same as having a social strategy. If nobody internally understands platform-specific content strategy, paid social, audience segmentation, creative testing, social analytics, conversion tracking, community growth or founder positioning, you may need specialist expertise before you need another employee.

A good social media marketing agency for startups should connect content activity with meaningful business outcomes.

08

How to Choose the Right Social Media Agency in London

Once you've decided to outsource, choosing the right partner becomes the next challenge.

Don't simply search for the "best social media agency London" and pick the one with the biggest follower count.

Look for an agency that understands your business model, audience and growth objectives.

Look Beyond Follower Growth

Followers can be useful, but they're not the end goal.

✓Qualified leads
✓Website traffic
✓Engagement quality
✓Conversion rates
✓Cost per lead
✓Content performance
✓Brand awareness
✓Revenue contribution

Ask How Performance Is Measured

A credible agency should be able to explain what success looks like before work begins.

If the reporting focuses entirely on impressions, likes and follower numbers, ask harder questions.

Check Strategic and Creative Capability

Your agency should be able to answer:

"Why are we creating this content?"

Not simply:

"What should we post this Tuesday?"

Strategy should come before the content calendar.

Make Sure the Agency Understands Your Audience

A London startup doesn't need generic social media advice copied from a global brand.

Your customer
Your market
Your competitors
Your positioning
Your sales process
Your business objectives

That context is what turns social media management into a growth function.

09

Final Verdict: When Should a London Startup Outsource Social Media?

Final Verdict

Build the Social Media Capability Your Startup Actually Needs.

There is no magic employee count or revenue figure that tells every London startup when to outsource social media.

The better question is whether your current setup is giving social media the expertise, consistency and strategic attention it needs to contribute to growth.

Is your current setup giving social media the expertise, consistency and strategic attention it needs to contribute to growth?

If the answer is no, you may already be at the right time.

For an early-stage startup, outsourcing can make sense when the founder is stretched, internal marketing resources are limited, content is inconsistent or specialist skills are missing.

For a more established startup with a mature marketing department, high content volume and social media as a core acquisition channel, building an internal team may eventually provide greater control and long-term value.

And for many businesses in between, the hybrid model can be the smartest option.

The goal isn't to choose between an agency and an employee.

The goal is to build the social media capability your startup needs without wasting time, budget or attention.

For London founders, that means knowing when social media has moved from something you should be doing to something you need to be doing properly.
